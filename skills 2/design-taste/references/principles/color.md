# Design Taste: Color

> Layer tags: `L1` foundation (always), `L2` tonal preset (only for that tone), `L3` brand-negotiable. `Rnnn` = the comparison round that produced the rule (see ../evidence.md). Non-negotiables in SKILL.md survive any brand override.

- **Go fully neutral or commit to a considered palette. Never use a lone token accent.** Type set entirely in neutrals is clean and acceptable. A multi-hue system applied structurally across several elements, including the ground, makes a piece feel finished and is preferred when color is used. Several hues are welcome. What fails is the in-between: a single colored text element on an otherwise neutral page reads as odd. `L1` · R033
- **Color in type marks hierarchy, and it goes to the same two moments as the special typefaces.** The primary hue goes on the headline. A secondary accent goes on the promoted practical information, such as the date. The subhead, body and metadata stay in the neutral ink. This mirrors the type system, where the display serif and text serif mark those same two elements, so face and color reinforce one structure. `L1` · R034
- **A hierarchy color must clearly read as a color.** Type color that sits so dark and desaturated that it nearly matches the neutral ink fails at its job, because the distinction disappears. Push the hue just far enough from the ink to be unmistakable, while keeping it restrained. `L1` · R035
- **Restrained saturation for quiet subjects.** Between muted and mid: clearly colored, never loud. `L2 · quiet/premium` · R035 (bolder briefs not yet tested)
- **Palette temperature follows the concept.** For neutral or institutional subjects, lead cool for trust with a warm accent on the promoted detail (R036). When the concept and imagery are themselves warm and inviting, such as food, gathering or hospitality, stay within one warm family: a warm lead with a related warm accent (R061). In every case, choose hues deliberately for their psychological associations and pull them from the imagery. The color-role rules still hold: one color per element, accent scarce, metadata neutral. `L2` · R036, R061
- **Color is spent only where it means something.** Hue goes to the headline and the promoted detail, and nowhere else. Metadata and other minor tiers stay in neutral gray. Don't extend the primary hue into tints and shades for lesser elements, and don't wash the ground in a pale tint of it. Coloring the metadata calls attention to things that don't need it. `L1` · R037
- **Contrast is a floor, then a mood dial.** Accessible contrast is the hard minimum. Above it, contrast is an expressive tool: secondary text such as body and metadata can be dimmed toward the floor to set a quieter, moodier tone, on light or dark grounds alike. The headline and the promoted detail keep full strength. `L1` · R038
- **Going dark means a colored field, not gray.** When a piece moves to a dark ground, the primary hue becomes the field: a deep version of the brand's cool color, not a neutral near-black. Type then flips to white, with the headline in white. Dark and light versions are separate color designs, not inversions. Color roles get reassigned for the dark ground rather than carried over unchanged. Whether to go dark depends on the imagery, for example a dim photograph. `L1` · R038, R039
- **The accent stays scarce.** The warm accent works because it occupies a small area, the one promoted detail. Moving it onto the headline, a large mass, floods the piece with warmth and loses the contrast that made it work. Accent area stays small relative to the primary and neutral areas, on light or dark grounds. `L1` · R040
- **The warm accent survives the move to dark.** On a deep cool field, keep the warm accent on the promoted detail. Its temperature contrast breaks up the sameness of white-on-blue, which a white or cool-shifted accent fails to do. `L1` · R039
- **Pull exact hues from the imagery. Never use near-duplicates.** When type or graphics share a hue family with the image or illustration, use the image's actual color rather than a slightly different one. Two blues that are almost the same read as a mistake, the color equivalent of a near-tangent. Either match exactly or contrast clearly. `L1` · R057
- **One element, one color.** *(Holds in playful work too: R057.)* Never split a color within a single element, for example coloring only the emphasized part of a headline. Weight already handles emphasis inside the headline, so color applies to the headline as a whole unit. `L1` · R034
- **Tint the background only when the imagery calls for it.** A tinted ground is a mood decision, and it should be drawn from, and judged against, the actual photograph's palette. Plain paper is the default. Tint when the image suggests it. `L2/L3` · R034
- **In interactive work, a lone text accent means "actionable."** One color singled out on text reads as a link or call to action, because that's the convention. Reserve that treatment for actionable items in web or UI contexts, and don't use it decoratively in print or static pieces. `L1` · R033
- **Never pure black on pure white.** Full black on full white vibrates. Use a warm off-black ink on an off-white ground, as the house palette does, or deep colored fields. `L1` · R083
- **Accessible contrast is non-negotiable.** Text must meet proper contrast-ratio standards against whatever is behind it. Color and placement decisions are made inside that limit, never at its expense. `L1` · **non-negotiable** · R009
