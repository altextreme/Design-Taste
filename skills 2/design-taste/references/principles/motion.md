# Design Taste: Motion

> Layer tags: `L1` foundation (always), `L2` tonal preset (only for that tone), `L3` brand-negotiable. `Rnnn` = the comparison round that produced the rule (see ../evidence.md). Non-negotiables in SKILL.md survive any brand override.

- **Always eased, never mechanical.** Every movement accelerates and decelerates. Linear or constant-speed motion is never used for design elements. `L1` · R041
- **Entrances ease out, with a gentle landing rather than a snap.** Elements arrive with momentum and decelerate smoothly into place. A slow start, as in ease-in or ease-in-out, reads as laggy. An exponential snap-stop reads as abrupt. The ideal is a moderate ease-out curve: responsive at the start and soft at the end. `L1` · R041, R042
- **Build in reading order, one meaning unit at a time.** Choreography is a sequential build that follows the reading chain: image, then label, headline, subhead, body, details. Each unit arrives in turn. But a unit that reads as one thought, such as a multi-line headline, enters as one piece, not line by line. Motion should respect the same semantic grouping the layout does. Everything arriving at once throws away the build. `L1` · R043
- **Elements enter with a fade and a gentle rise.** A short upward drift paired with a fade-in reads as elegant and suits quiet, refined pieces. Mask or wipe reveals and motionless fades are less fitting for this register. `L2 · quiet/premium` · R044 (other tones may call for different entries)
- **An exit is chosen by what comes next.** Exits serve continuity, not symmetry for its own sake.
  - If another frame follows with the same directional language, continue the motion: elements leave in the order they arrived, still traveling in the entry direction, so the next frame's build picks up the same vector.
  - If the piece is a single standalone frame, mirror the entry: reverse order, reverse direction.
  - Everything vanishing at once is the weakest choice in either case.

  Exits accelerate away, the counterpart of ease-out entrances. `L1` · R045
- **Always hold long enough to read everything.** A frame must stay fully resolved on screen long enough for an average reader to read every word in it, body copy included, before anything exits. This is **non-negotiable**, a legibility requirement in the same class as accessible contrast. Brisk motion applies to how things move, never to how long they stay. *Implication (provisional):* if the required hold makes a frame drag, reduce the copy on that frame or split it across frames. Don't shorten the hold. `L1` · **non-negotiable** · R046
- **Keep long holds alive with a slow push-in.** While type rests for its full reading time, the image can carry a slow, continuous push-in (the Ken Burns move), eased at both ends. It adds life without competing with reading. Anchor the push so image features move away from overlaid text rather than toward it. `L1` · R047
- **The camera only travels to reveal something.** A lateral pan or drift is justified only when it uncovers more of the story, for example more of the room the light falls on. A drift for its own sake is decoration. This is the motion counterpart of cropping for the concept. `L1` · R020, R047
- **Direct motion, with no cartoon principles, by default.** Elements travel straight to their resting place and stop. No anticipation dips, no overshoot, no follow-through settle. Classic animation exaggeration reads as less elegant in quiet and energetic work. Bounce and spring are fine when the concept is genuinely playful. `L1` default · `L2 · playful` exception · R041, R048, R058
- **Motion flexes with the concept.** The motion dials move with tone the same way the type and color dials do:
  - **Calm/quiet:** eased fade-and-rise and a gentle build.
  - **Energetic:** faster timing, bigger travel, crisp wipe and mask reveals and tighter stagger. Still eased, still no bounce.
  - **Playful:** spring and bounce allowed.

  The structural motion rules hold in every register: reading-order build, the headline as one unit, reading-time holds, exits that hand off, and steady tempo. `L2` · R044, R058
- **Nothing exits unless something follows.** A single piece of content, looping or not, builds in once and then stays, kept alive by slow ambient camera motion that breathes in and out. Exits exist only to hand off to what comes next. Building out and back in with nothing new to show is wasted motion. (This refines R045: the mirrored exit applies only when a standalone piece must end, such as at a cut in a longer edit.) `L1` · R045, R049
- **In sequences, frames hand off with motion.** Outgoing content exits in the direction of travel and incoming content builds in on the same vector, so the sequence reads as one continuous, considered movement. Hard cuts and slow crossfades read as default and unconsidered by comparison. Motion's primary job is to connect, carrying the eye from one idea to the next. Ambient motion such as the push-in keeps holds alive. `L1` · R045, R050
- **Pacing equals reading time plus brisk motion.** Transitions are quick and smooth, holds are as long as the copy requires, and nothing lingers in transition. `L1` · R041, R046, R050
- **Let the concept inform the hero motion.** Where the subject offers a motion idea, the hero element's entrance can interpret it, literally (light sweeping in to reveal) or metaphorically (type brightening like dawn). These creative interpretations are preferred to generic motion, with a slight lean toward the subtler, metaphorical reading. The house fade-and-rise is the fallback when the concept offers nothing, and supporting elements keep the house language either way. `L1` · R051
- **Contrast rules apply to resolved states.** A brief, deliberate low-contrast moment during an entrance, such as type brightening from faint, is acceptable. Every element must reach full accessible contrast before its reading hold begins. `L1` · R046, R051
- **Black is for structure, not transitions.** Fade in from black at the start and out to black at the end, or dip to black at a genuine break in the story. Dipping through black between every frame is a cheat. Frames hand off with motion instead (R050). `L1` · R050, R082
- **Keep tempo steady.** Every transition in a sequence moves at the same pace. Accelerations and deliberate tempo shifts are unnecessary complication, unless the motion is synchronized to music, in which case the music sets the tempo. `L1` · R052
- **Smooth, but brisk.** The character is soft, eased motion without the slow drift: gentle curves with a confident, moderate pace. Snappy, hard-stopping motion feels abrupt, and long, floating motion takes too long. No physics bounce or spring overshoot by default. `L1` · R041 (default character; tone presets may shift pace)
