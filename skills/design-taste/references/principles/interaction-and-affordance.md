# Design Taste: Interaction & Affordance

> Layer tags: `L1` foundation (always), `L2` tonal preset (only for that tone), `L3` brand-negotiable. `Rnnn` = the comparison round that produced the rule (see ../evidence.md). Non-negotiables in SKILL.md survive any brand override.

- **Colored text signals interactivity.** In UI and web work, a single accent color on text is reserved for links and actionable items, and isn't spent on emphasis or decoration. `L1` · R033, R076
- **Underline ordinary text links inside paragraphs at rest.** They need a distinct link color and underline before hover. Navigation is different: leave the inactive labels un-underlined and mark only the active item with a line. A linked wordmark needs no underline. A link styled as a button needs no text underline because the button treatment signals the action. `L1` · **non-negotiable for paragraph links in UI** · R076, R155
- **State changes transition, never just swap.** Hover and press states should animate between states with a quick eased tone transition (state feedback; the strong ease-out applies to surfaces that open, R164). An instant color-only change on a button is too plain. Purposeful motion, like a fill sweeping in or an arrow nudging toward the destination, is welcome. `L1` · R076
- **Pressing a simple control changes its tone; it does not shrink.** A row, button or toggle shows the press with a tone or fill change on the house transition. A scale-down that imitates a physical button being pushed is an "unnecessary bell and whistle" for everyday interactions. *Scope:* his words were "for simple interactions like this"; an expressive or playful control is not covered. `L1` · R170 (queue 15; one round)
- **Elevation is a legitimate interactive affordance.** On the web, lifting a card toward the viewer on hover, with a slight rise, scale and shadow, literally pulls it out of the page and is a strong signal for cards and clickable surfaces. This is the UI exception to the no-shadow default, because there the shadow communicates state rather than decorating. Match the treatment to what is being activated. `L1` · R015, R064, R076
- **Hover amplifies; it never gates what a visitor needs to decide.** Anything that helps someone choose (price, length, what is included, the next action) is visible at rest, and hover only adds polish such as a small lift. *Why:* options cannot be compared if they cannot be seen at once; touch and keyboard users have no hover, so gated content is simply lost to them; hidden content makes the visitor hunt, and effort and doubt rise before they have a reason to care; and a page that changes under the pointer is less calm than one that holds still. *Condition that flips it:* if the extra is truly optional (not needed to decide), a hover reveal is fine. *Confirmed in context (R161, a working billing page):* details visible at rest beat details on hover, for the touch-and-keyboard, hunting and calm reasons; comparing side by side was not ticked this time. `L1` · R156, R161
- **Anything opened by interaction opens once and stays until the person closes it.** Close it with a second click, a click elsewhere, or Escape, never by pointer drift. *Why:* content in a panel that vanishes while you aim for it cannot be read or reached; persistence lets the person decide when they are done, which feels in control; and click-to-open behaves the same on touch, mouse and keyboard, so there is one behavior to learn. *Confirmed in context (R161, a live filter panel):* ticked that several filters are set in one visit so the panel must stay, that a panel closing on drift is hard to use, and that click is one behavior everywhere. **Menus open on click only, even on devices that hover.** In R161 click-only beat hover-opens-then-stays and hover-opens-closes-on-leave for a top navigation menu. *Why:* click is predictable and the same on every device, and it sets the precedent that a menu is reached the same way everywhere. *Not ticked:* hover saves a click; navigation menus open on hover by convention; a menu opening as you pass is annoying; a menu that stays after you leave feels stuck. This overturns his earlier stated hover-open convenience. `L1` · R156, R161
- **Tooltips warm up: the first label waits, its neighbours open at once with no fade.** Once one tooltip in a row is showing, moving to the next shows its label immediately; the delay comes back after a short pause away from the row. *Why:* the first wait matters because it stops labels flashing as the pointer passes, and after that it is only in the way; once someone is exploring a toolbar, waiting again for each label is slow; instant neighbours feel like the interface is keeping up. *Not ticked:* the same wait every time is consistent and calmer; instant labels feel jumpy; visible text labels instead of icon tooltips. *No flip condition given.* `L1` · R161
- **When a dragged surface is let go, honour what the hand meant.** A short fast flick commits (a sheet closes) even when it has moved only a little, and dragging past a boundary gives way with rising resistance instead of stopping dead; either way it settles without overshoot. *Why:* a quick flick is what the hand meant; a soft edge says the limit has been reached without feeling broken; a hard stop feels like the surface is stuck. *Not ticked:* a fixed distance is predictable; a flick risks closing by accident; resistance is a gimmick. *No flip condition given.* `L1` · R162
- **A motion that is triggered again mid-way turns around from where it is.** A second click on a sliding panel (or a toggle, an expand and collapse) reverses from the panel's current position at once; it never jumps to the end first, and it never finishes the first move before reversing. *Why:* turning around at once feels like the interface is listening; a jump reads as a glitch; finishing first feels like lag, as if the click was ignored. *Also ticked:* he rarely clicks this fast, so it matters little to him personally; treat it as a quality floor, not a headline. *Not ticked:* finishing first is calmer; the panel should always be where the last click asked. *No flip condition given.* `L1` · R163
- **A confirmation appears at the cause or at the consequence, and stays until it has been read.** Beside the control that caused it (a Save button), in the place of the thing that changed (an archived row, with Undo), or at the top of the page the person lands on (after a permanent delete). **Use a toast only when the cause has left the screen and there is no consequence to point at**, such as "Link copied" after a menu closes. *Why:* the message should appear where the cause is, or where the person lands; a message that stays is always seen; a toast can vanish before it is read or before the person can undo; showing the change in the row's place shows what changed and where it went; and a permanent action deserves a message that stays. *Beat:* the toast with Undo, which was a stated preference, for save, archive and delete. *Held:* the toast for a quick action whose cause is gone, where a changed label was easy to miss and the toast is the familiar pattern. *Not ticked:* that a toast does not disturb the layout, and that a toast gives Undo a time-limited place. Undo lives in place, in the row. *No flip condition given.* `L1` · R159, R160
