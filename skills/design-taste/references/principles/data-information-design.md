# Design Taste: Data / Information Design

> Layer tags: `L1` foundation (always), `L2` tonal preset (only for that tone), `L3` brand-negotiable. `Rnnn` = the comparison round that produced the rule (see ../evidence.md). Non-negotiables in SKILL.md survive any brand override.

- **Conventional, clear charts by default.** A proper axis, light gridlines, axis labels and every category labeled. This reads as the cleanest. Ultra-minimal data-ink that strips the axis is less preferred, and so is decorating every mark. Keep values off individual bars unless they're being called out. `L1` · R074
- **Form follows the message and the attention budget.**
  - **A trend:** show a progression over time with a conventional chart.
  - **The headline is one metric:** lead with one big number and a short supporting line. It's the fastest read with the least cognitive load, and the default for quick-hit contexts like social posts.
  - **Time allows:** sequence the two in motion, showing the chart and then transitioning to the big number to land the point.
  - **Pictograms:** use them sparingly. Many small repeated icons overwhelm and read as visual noise, like "a bunch of tiny bugs."

  `L1` · R075
- **Optically center a label beside a big number.** When a large figure sits beside a multi-line label, center the label block vertically on the figure's optical height, from cap or figure top to baseline. Don't baseline-align them, and don't center on the line box: either way the label sinks below the number. The same applies to any large element paired with smaller text. `L1` · V01
- **Decorate only to tell the story, and only once.** If one data point matters to the narrative, such as a record year, treat that single mark differently: a highlight color, an icon or a direct label. Decorating every mark makes nothing important, the same hierarchy logic as everywhere else. `L1` · R074
