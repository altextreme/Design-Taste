# Design Taste: Contrast & Hierarchy

> Layer tags: `L1` foundation (always), `L2` tonal preset (only for that tone), `L3` brand-negotiable. `Rnnn` = the comparison round that produced the rule (see ../evidence.md). Non-negotiables in SKILL.md survive any brand override.

- **A strong second voice is welcome. A tie is not.** A headline can be pushed up to nearly rival the image, which adds interest, but the image should still edge it out. When the headline grows, give the image a little more height to keep it in the lead. Two focal points work as a clear first and a close second, not as equals. `L1` · R016, R017
- **Emphasis follows the piece's job.** Functional information the audience needs to act on, like dates on a flyer, deserves more emphasis than its default rank as a detail. Promote it in proportion: enough to be found quickly, never enough to compete with the headline. `L1` · R016, R017
- **Promote by size, not by relocation.** Raise important information by giving it a moderate size step within its natural place in the reading order, for example the date on its own line in the footer. A smaller bump that keeps it aligned in the detail row is also acceptable. Don't move it somewhere more prominent but out of sequence. `L1` · R017
- **Hierarchy is enforced, not discovered.** The viewer should never wonder what to read first. Rank between levels should be unmistakable, and the spatial order should match the rank order. This held in every tonal register tested (quiet, playful, energetic, luxury, warm): tone changes the voice, never the enforcement. `L1` · R001, R002, R056–R061
