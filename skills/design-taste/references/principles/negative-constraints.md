# Design Taste: Negative Constraints

> Layer tags: `L1` foundation (always), `L2` tonal preset (only for that tone), `L3` brand-negotiable. `Rnnn` = the comparison round that produced the rule (see ../evidence.md). Non-negotiables in SKILL.md survive any brand override.

- **Heavy condensed display type on a quiet or contemplative subject.** It reads as comical. Called "horrible". R011
- **Robotic, mechanical serifs.** Overly engineered or uniform serifs that lack warmth. R026
- **Fully justified text.** "I see no reason to fully justify text in any of my designs." R030
- **Shadow-only legibility fixes.** Drop shadows on type over a busy photo, used instead of a scrim or panel. Extremely difficult to read. R064, R071
- **Glyph collisions between lines.** Descenders hitting ascenders or i-dots in the next line, such as a *Q* tail striking the dot of an *i* or the top of a *t*. R025, R029
- **Dated effects: bevel and emboss, drop-shadowed extruded type.** Feels very dated. Also, static glows and shadows used as decoration. R064
- **Dense pictograms.** Rows of many tiny repeated icons. Overwhelming; reads like bugs on the page. R075
- **Decorated charts.** Gradient-filled bars (especially warm-to-cool gradients on data marks), an icon on every bar, a value on every bar. Overkill noise. R074
- **Decorative motifs and background patterns.** Marks centered on rules, corner ornaments, and repeating patterns behind text. Distracting, noisy clutter. R069
- **Faked photographic effects.** Simulated depth of field or blur added in post. R067
- **Amateur imagery for professional subjects.** Snapshot-quality photos with cheap phone-camera rendering in a piece about professional creative work. R065
- **Motion clichés, never used:** elastic drop-in bounces, spin-ins, slow dramatic zooms with lens-flare or light-leak overlays, attention shakes and wiggles, word-by-word scale pops, and dipping to black between every transition. These differ from what is accepted: a subtle spring is fine for genuinely playful concepts (R058), and a slow push-in on a hold is preferred (R047). The clichéd versions are the gratuitous, effect-for-effect's-sake forms. R082
- **Motion clichés, only when the concept truly calls for them:** typewriter reveals and scramble or decode effects. Never in quiet or refined work. R082
- **Color combinations to avoid:**
  - **Vibrating complements:** equally saturated opposing hues side by side.
  - **Muddy, murky, low-contrast palettes:** these are an accessibility failure.
  - **Pure black on pure white:** too vibrating.
  - **Blush pink and sage:** avoid entirely.

  R083
- **Color combinations only on explicit request:**
  - **Holiday red and green:** only when a holiday piece is explicitly asked for, and even then used tastefully.
  - **Rainbow gradients:** only when explicitly asked for.
  - **Teal and orange:** only as a requested color grade on footage or photography. Never as background and type colors.

  R083
- **Use sparingly:** tech purple-to-pink gradients. R083
- **Typographic crimes, never:** faux bold or italic (software-synthesized styles), stretched or squished letterforms, straight quotes and hyphens standing in for real quotes and dashes, widows and orphans, rivers in justified text, wide letterspacing on lowercase, too many typefaces in one piece, long centered paragraphs, all-caps body copy, cramped body leading, and small text on a busy image with no support. "Avoid all of these. These are really bad." R084
- **Outlined text only from the font itself.** Hollow or stroked type is occasionally acceptable when it comes from a typeface designed with an outline style. A stroke faked by the software is never acceptable, in line with the no-simulated-effects rule. R084
- **Generic, machine-made tells, never:**
  - Floating glossy 3D shapes.
  - Emoji where a proper icon belongs. Never use emoji as bullets or markers; use real icons drawn to the house icon rules.
  - Pill-shaped buttons and tags used everywhere.
  - Generic stock 3D character illustrations.
  - Over-rounded corners on everything.

  R085
- **Acceptable only on explicit request:** fully centered layouts, bento grids, icon-in-circle feature rows, gradient-blob heroes, glassmorphism, sparkle icons with hype copy, and gradient-filled headline text. Never reach for them by default. R085
- **Simulated paper texture.** Fake paper tooth or fiber as a background effect. R062
- **Italic emphasis in display serifs.** Collisions between ascenders and descenders, and a general preference for weight-based emphasis. R025
- **Display faces with quirky, idiosyncratic letterforms.** Odd glyph shapes that hurt readability, especially in letters like *h* and *f*. R011
- **Hairline Didone display serifs, outside luxury.** Extreme thick-thin contrast that makes text "extremely difficult to read" in quiet or editorial work (R012). The exception is luxury, where a Didone at large display size on a dark field is preferred (R060). Never use one at small sizes. R012, R060
- **Near-tangents.** Elements that almost touch, especially corner to corner, such as a label's last character nearly kissing a window corner in the image. R020

---
- **Text block centered over the middle of a full-bleed photo.** It obscures the image "in a horrible way". R098
- **Tabs across the top as primary navigation on mobile.** Off the table. R101
- **A web page on one flat ground, sections divided only by hairlines.** Feels like the lazy way out. R104
- **A solid caption panel on video.** Too low fidelity; looks like the native Instagram/TikTok text tool. R109
- **A two-letter monogram as the logo mark.** The lazy way out; a strong wordmark is better. R113
- **An email with no brand header.** Unacceptable: it fails to establish the brand, which is a core job of the piece. R115
- **Extreme wide-crop images (banner strips) in email content blocks.** Overwhelming; the images dominate and distract from the text. R116
- **A table drawn as a full grid of cell borders.** Dated, spreadsheet-like default. R117
- **A video end card with only a logo (a dead end).** Useless; there is no next step. R122
- **A "More" drop-down in primary navigation.** Hides important sections and deprioritizes them. R126
- **Story text placed over the photo's actual focal point.** Obscures it and muddies the whole piece. R134
- **A poster with a huge title and the date and time lost in small print, or one with no size hierarchy at all.** Both are off the table. R138
- **A label that splits the logo mark and the wordmark apart.** The two halves of the logo always appear together to build recall and salience. R139
