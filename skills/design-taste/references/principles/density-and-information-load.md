# Design Taste: Density & Information Load

> Layer tags: `L1` foundation (always), `L2` tonal preset (only for that tone), `L3` brand-negotiable. `Rnnn` = the comparison round that produced the rule (see ../evidence.md). Non-negotiables in SKILL.md survive any brand override.

- **Do not cut the name that identifies a row; make room for it first.** If the screen has space, widen the column. For names that differ only near their end (versions, dates, file types), wrap onto a second line rather than cut. If a cut is truly unavoidable, cut in the middle so the distinguishing end stays visible, and reveal the full name in a tooltip after a short pause on hover, and on keyboard focus and tap (R156). Never cut such names at the end. *Why:* an end cut hides exactly the part that tells the files apart; a middle cut keeps the version and file type visible; his note: there was clearly enough room to make the table wider. *Not ticked:* that a name should never be cut at all; that wrapping makes rows uneven and harder to scan; that a middle cut looks odd; that one line per row is calmer. *Condition:* wrap is his preference for file names like these; the middle cut with a tooltip is the fallback when width is fixed. `L1` · R162
- **Density is set by the channel and the time available.**
  - **Glance media** (banner ads, social posts) get one message and minimal information. There are a second or two to win attention, so be direct and light.
  - **Posters and general pieces** sit in the moderate middle.
  - **Reference media** (itineraries, emails, programs) can carry dense, multi-message content, organized by the same hierarchy.

  There's a place for each. Pick by the channel and application. `L1` · R075, R077
- **Busy is often just one element too many.** Richness tips into busyness when the count of competing items passes what the hierarchy can organize. In a lead-plus-support grid, a lead and two supporting items reads as rich. Adding a fourth item of similar weight reads as busy. When a set feels crowded, cut an item before shrinking everything. `L1` · R021 (partial)
